How it decides

Every decision on this page is made by fixed rules in code, so the same availability always gives the same roster. It is the logic from the Problem C Python notebook, rewritten in JavaScript so it runs instantly in the browser. The Tests tab checks the two agree.

The café's rules

  1. Every shift is covered by exactly one person.
  2. A person can only work a shift that sits entirely inside their hours that day.
  3. No one works more than 5 days a week. Any shift on a day counts as a day worked, so a double is one day.
  4. No one works more than 2 shifts a day, and two shifts must be back-to-back: Morning + Afternoon or Afternoon + Evening, never Morning + Evening.

How it builds the roster

  1. For each day, list every way the three shifts could be shared out under rules 1, 2 and 4.
  2. Work through the week a day at a time, trying those options.
  3. Skip any option that would take someone past 5 days. If a day has no option left, step back and try something different on an earlier day.
  4. Keep the most even roster found: first the fewest shifts given to the busiest person, then the smallest gap between busiest and least busy.
  5. Stop early once it finds a perfect split. With 21 shifts and 3 staff, that's 7 each.
  6. Check the finished roster against every rule again before showing it.

When there's no valid roster

The page says why, checking in this order:

  • Nobody free for a shift: no one's hours cover the whole of it.
  • A day can't be split: each shift has someone, but only by giving one person 3 shifts or a Morning + Evening.
  • The week can't fit: every day works on its own, but someone would need more than 5 days.

Cover a shift

Each other staff member is checked in this order. The first rule that stops them is the reason shown.

  1. Not working that day (day off).
  2. Their hours don't cover the whole shift.
  3. It would be a 3rd shift that day.
  4. It wouldn't be back-to-back with the shift they already have.
  5. It would be a 6th day this week.

Everyone else's shifts stay where they are. People who can cover are listed in staff order, not ranked.

What it doesn't do

  • It balances the number of shifts, not hours. A 5-hour weekday morning counts the same as a 4.5-hour evening.
  • It doesn't know about preferences, skills, wages, award rules, breaks or how long someone has between shifts.
  • Only availability can be changed here. The 5-day and 2-shift limits come from the café's spreadsheet.
  • When nobody can cover a shift directly, it doesn't reshuffle the rest of the week to make room. The manager decides what happens next.
  • A roster that follows the rules isn't automatically a fair one for each person. The manager still checks it.
